Using ESA or charter school funds?

A plain guide if you buy curriculum with a charter school allotment or a state education savings account. Your program decides.

Many homeschool families use a charter school allotment or a state education savings account (ESA) to buy curriculum. If that is how you buy books, start here, then check with your program or your teacher before you spend the funds.

Level Up Makers is a computer science and game design curriculum. The manual, Level Up: Make Your First Game with AI, is written for students ages 9–13 and built on free Microsoft MakeCode Arcade. It may qualify. Each program makes its own decision, so we can't guarantee eligibility.

Option 1: Amazon paperback

The paperback is $34.99. It is sold and shipped by Amazon.

Many charter schools and ESA programs order through Amazon or Amazon Business. You can ask your program to order it. Where your program allows reimbursement, you can also buy it on Amazon and keep the itemized invoice. On Amazon, open Your Orders, open the order, and choose Invoice.

Some programs only allow purchases from their own marketplace. Some programs in Texas work that way. If yours does, an Amazon order may not count. Ask your program before you buy.

What to tell your reviewer

You can share this with your reviewer. Your program still decides.

  • Subject area. Computer science / technology. The site describes it as a homeschool coding curriculum.
  • Grade band. 4–7 (ages 9–12). The book page says the manual is written for students ages 9–13.
  • Format. A structured, self-paced curriculum with 10 levels, a short Parent Guide, and printables. The complete manual is 178 pages. A parent or guardian runs the lessons.
  • Tool. It uses free, browser-based MakeCode Arcade, so no software purchase is needed. A child does not need an account to start.
  • What it is. An instructional curriculum. Students make a game as the project. You are not buying a video game.
