What Your Child Builds in 10 Levels: From First Sprite to Finished Game

"Learn to make games" can mean almost anything. So here's exactly what a student does, level by level, in Level Up: Make Your First Game with AI.

Each level is a short session with a clear goal. By the end, your child has one original platform game that friends and family can play.

Level 1: Spark!

Your child invents an original game idea, draws their own hero and builds a tiny game they can actually play. The first rule of game makers comes up right away: borrow the feeling of a game you love, not the character.

Level 2: Rules of the Game

Before building more, students look at how games are put together. They learn the "core loop," the simple cycle of actions that makes a game fun to play again and again.

Level 3: Hero & World Builder

Time to become a pixel artist. Your child learns some pixel art basics, sketches on paper first, then brings the hero and the game world to life in MakeCode.

Level 4: Make It Move

This is where it starts to feel like a real platformer. Students add gravity, jumping and a world that scrolls as the hero moves. They also learn a good habit early: back up your game before big changes.

Level 5: Danger Zone

Enemies and obstacles arrive. Before adding them, students think about what makes a challenge fair or unfair, and they draw a difficulty curve to plan it.

Level 6: Level Designer

One level becomes three, from easy to hard, connected like a real adventure. Students use a simple pattern for each level: teach a skill, test it, then add a twist.

Level 7: Juice It Up

Sounds, effects, a title screen and the small touches that make a game feel good to play. Game designers call this "game feel," and students love this part.

Level 8: Playtest Party

Your child watches other people play the game, takes notes and fixes the bugs they find. It's a lesson in patience and in listening, and the game gets better every round.

Level 9: Choose Your Next World

With one game done, students try a second tool and rebuild their first game in it. It shows them that the skills they learned carry over.

Level 10: Ship It (Safely)

The finish line. Your child works through a short checklist with you covering privacy and more, and then, only with your OK, shares the finished game.

Extras along the way

The guide also includes:

  • A Parent Guide and clearly marked grown-up steps
  • Optional "AI Sidekick" prompts for your own AI account, with you typing
  • An optional poster side quest using Canva
  • Printable sheets: hero card, game idea sheet, pixel grid, world map, level planner, playtest scorecard, sharing checklist and level badges

No coding experience is needed. The steps start at the very beginning.
