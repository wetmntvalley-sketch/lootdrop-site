What Level Up is

Level Up: Make Your First Game with AI is the first Level Up Makers manual. It is written for students ages 9–13. A parent or guardian runs the lessons and can pre-order the full manual. Each level is a quest with a goal, a hands-on activity, an AI prompt you type yourself, a boss challenge, and a parent note.

The main tool is Microsoft MakeCode Arcade. It is free, it runs in the browser, and a child does not need an account to start building. Blocks make a character move, pick up an item, and race a timer. Signing in is only for cloud saving. The manual tells you to download a backup, because clearing browser data can erase a project.

The rule of the book: AI is your sidekick, not your controller. You type. Your student decides what to keep. Every prompt is written so it works in any chat AI.

What students make

Original ideas and small games they can actually play. Famous characters stay out of anything they share.

In free Level 1, about 60–90 minutes

A one-page game idea, a hero they drew, and a tiny “collect it” game: move, grab the item, score points, beat the clock. Then someone in the family playtests it.

Across the full manual

Paper rules, pixel heroes and a world, a mini platformer, fair enemies, three levels, sound and score, a family playtest, the same game in a second tool, and a cover screen with credits.

The Learning Path

Three manuals, in order. Students start by making a first game, then learn the kind of tools behind the games they already play.

Growing toward real games. Godot is a full game engine that indie studios use to ship commercial games. It is free, it does not ask for an account, and the games your student makes are theirs.

Safety for the later steps

  • No Godot account for your child. You install it. Opening the editor does not ask for your child’s name, email, or a login.
  • The AI voice lesson is yours to run. OpenAI doesn’t allow children under 13 to use voice directly. You talk or type. Your child writes the character, listens, and decides what to keep. Their own voice stays out of it.
  • itch.io goes through you. Publishing uses the parent’s account, with comments off. Use the made-up creator name.

Ten levels

These are the lessons inside Level Up 1. Level 1 is on the site now. Levels 2–10 are the plan for that manual. Each AI prompt is for you to type on your own account.

  1. Level 1 Spark · free now

    Goal. Invent an original game idea and make a tiny playable game.

    Activity. Idea Forge, then a 30-second “collect it” game in MakeCode Arcade. Read the chapter.

    AI prompt you type. Help brainstorm 10 original hero ideas that are not from any existing game.

  2. Level 2 Rules of the Game

    Goal. Understand goals, rules, win and lose, and the core loop.

    Activity. A paper prototype played with dice or cards.

    AI prompt you type. Explain a core loop to an 11-year-old using 3 games as examples, without spoilers.

  3. Level 3 Hero & World Builder

    Goal. Create original characters and a world.

    Activity. Draw 16×16 pixel heroes and fill in a world map sheet.

    AI prompt you type. Give feedback on this character idea. Is it too close to any famous character?

  4. Level 4 Make It Move

    Goal. Controls, speed, jumping, and gravity.

    Activity. Build a mini platformer in MakeCode Arcade.

    AI prompt you type. My character falls through the floor in MakeCode Arcade. What are 3 things to check?

  5. Level 5 Danger Zone

    Goal. Enemies, obstacles, and a fair difficulty curve.

    Activity. Add 2 enemies and 3 difficulty steps.

    AI prompt you type. Suggest 5 enemy behaviors for a student’s game that are fun and not scary.

  6. Level 6 Level Designer

    Goal. Build maps with tilemaps, from easy to hard.

    Activity. Design 3 levels on graph paper, then build them.

    AI prompt you type. Review my level plan: is the jump from level 1 to 2 too hard?

  7. Level 7 Juice It Up

    Goal. Sound, effects, a score, and game feel.

    Activity. Add sounds, a score, a title screen, and a game-over screen.

    AI prompt you type. What small effects make collecting a coin feel satisfying?

  8. Level 8 Playtest Party

    Goal. Get feedback and squash bugs.

    Activity. Run a family playtest with the Playtest Scorecard.

    AI prompt you type. Turn these playtest notes into a top-5 fix list for a student.

  9. Level 9 Choose Your Next World

    Goal. Try a second tool: Scratch or GDevelop.

    Activity. Rebuild the Level 1 game in the new tool. You stay with them.

    AI prompt you type. Compare how Scratch and MakeCode Arcade handle the same “collect it” game.

  10. Level 10 Ship It (Safely)

    Goal. Share a game safely and proudly.

    Activity. The “Ready to Share?” checklist, credits, and a cover screen.

    AI prompt you type. Help write a short, friendly game description with no personal info.

How this stays safe

You are in the loop the whole time. These pages do not ask your child for anything.

No children’s personal info

Games, project titles, and AI chats use a made-up creator name. Real names, school, town, age, photos, and voice stay out.

AI on a parent’s account

Most chat AIs are not for under-13s to use on their own. You open the chat, you type, and you sit together. Your child never makes their own AI account for this manual.

Original characters only

Children name the action (jump, collect, race) and the feeling (fast, brave, silly), then invent someone new. Anything shared uses their own characters, names, art, and music.

Safe sharing

Level 1 stays on your computer. Public posting waits for Level 10, a parent, and the Ready to Share checklist. Family and friends come first. Comments and chat stay off or supervised.

These pages have no signup form, no analytics, and no embedded players or ads. We do not collect children’s information here. When the rest of the manual is ready, it will show on this page. There is no email list to join.
